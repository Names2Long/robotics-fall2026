What stood out to me was the difference between reading a models answer and checking what happened. When the model said the cup was empty, the sentence sounded like a report from a robot that had inspected it. Looking back at the setup, I realized the model had only received a text description. That made me ask a question I want to keep using in robotics, what evidence would the robot actually need before it could make that claim?
The maze was the most surprising part for me. I expected that naming the exact turn would help. Instead, the robot reached (1,1) and repeated the same blocked move and pause as before. Watching that happen made the action record more useful to me than the wording of the prompt. I could point to the exact place where the instruction and the behavior stopped matching.

The activity made me more interested in testing how robots use sensor information and feedback. For a robot working around people, saying “I still need to inspect that” can be more useful than giving a confident guess. I would want to design a system that makes it clear when it is proposing a plan, when it has evidence, and when an action has actually succeeded.
