# Lab 7 overview

Initial preflight checks installed robot software and the built workspace without starting Gazebo. A local guide without ROS explicitly limits its readiness claim to the image activities. Simulation startup and the live camera, clock and guard check are the opening section of Mission 2, not a separate page. Mission 3 repeats live checks after each world reset.

Students evaluate how a robot might locate a mug among desk equipment, then connect live camera evidence to rotation and route following in ROS 2 Jazzy and Gazebo. The work develops image-processing intuition, interpretation of learned outputs, matched comparisons, proportional steering and evidence-based deployment limits. It does not include model training.

Preflight verifies the complete image collection, actual model execution and saving, and prepares reusable predictions. The 16-step playground reveals one concept at a time, including a shared-input four-operation morphology comparison. Its final page supplies a documented executable Python reference for every step. Pixel clicks synchronize coordinate controls and a color swatch. Resize and crop are separate. Each lesson starts from the same source image or an explicit fixed mask. The final geometry lesson uses an identified manual silhouette rather than claiming detection success.

One detector investigation has three parts. Part A configures the supplied classical code and inspects its behavior on all shared conditions. Part B explains frozen weights and the different meanings of whole-image classification, cup detection and class-agnostic foreground segmentation. Part C starts with a three-condition paired count table and identical scenes shown side by side. Its questions refer directly to those outputs and one common mask example. Explained optional detail views retain threshold sweeps, box precision-recall/AP50, classification and mask metrics without requiring students to interpret every result at once.

Mission 2 searches for four colored pillars using HSV masks, largest-contour centroids and a turn-only controller. Mission 3 uses a downward camera, region of interest and proportional steering on the original straight and curved track artwork. New world launches reset the robot. A live preflight observes actual camera and clock messages and verifies the bridge and independent guard. Students retain recorded attempts with diagnostic images and timestamped commands. Missing frames and line loss stop motion. Interrupted attempts are valid evidence, not a reason to erase work.

Mission 1 uses bottom back/next buttons instead of a part selector. Part A needs both written responses before Part B or C can open. Mission 3 uses editable Python rather than sliders, with separate practice and track instructions and import panels. Its deliberately weak starter gain encourages diagnosis and controlled experiments. The ROS node executes the saved steering function and records its source. The independent safeguards remain fixed.

One final technical synthesis connects the three missions. There is no separate deployment readiness checklist. The course reflection uses the same five prompts and 300-word limit as earlier labs. Navigation, autosave, recovery files, draft backups and regenerated verified exports remain available. Original artwork attribution is in assets/robot_worlds/README.md.

The CC0 multi-object desk photograph and manually defined visible-mug annotations are documented in assets/conditions/TABLETOP_SOURCE.md. Two independent photographs supplement controlled transformations, which are not new physical camera views. The collection does not establish physical grasp safety, population-level generalization or demographic fairness.
