A suitable route must be practical to compute, executable by the robot and considerate of nearby people. The three missions showed that no single measurement establishes all three qualities.

In Mission 1, BFS and A* both found a 13.5 m shortest path on the open map, but A* expanded 700 nodes compared with BFSs 928. The heuristic reduced search work without changing optimal path length. On the constrained map, DFS expanded fewer nodes than BFS but produced a 104.0 m route instead of 24.5 m. IDDFS reached its expansion budget, which meant the search was unfinished, not that no route existed. Search efficiency and route quality therefore need separate evaluation.

Mission 2 showed that generating a valid route does not guarantee successful execution. Constrained map BFS succeeded in 90.7 simulation seconds over 23.946 m, while DFS failed after 30.988 seconds and only 6.944 m of travel. Nav2 succeeded on both maps, taking 35.0 seconds on the open map and 73.5 seconds on the constrained map, compared with BFSs 46.7 and 90.7 seconds. Its shorter journeys made it promising for delivery, although one successful trial per map does not establish reliability.

Mission 3 added personal space trade offs. On the open map, A* social_2 increased minimum person clearance from approximately 0.701 m to 0.752 m and reduced exposure below the chosen 0.75 m threshold from 1.8 seconds to zero. RRT social_1 succeeded with 1.758 m person clearance in 43.4 seconds, but its estimated wall clearance was only 0.134 m, compared with 0.622 m for A* social_2. More space around a person did not mean more space from every obstacle.

These measurements have limits. Travel distance, tracking error and clearance use estimated robot poses rather than simulator ground truth. Lidar range is a proximity indicator, not proof of physical contact. The personal space threshold is a scenario choice, not a universal comfort standard. A suitable real world route would require repeated testing, fresh obstacle and person information, and appropriate responses to moving people, blocked passages and uncertain sensing.
